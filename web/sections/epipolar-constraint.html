The Epipolar Constraint

Notation and Setup

Given two cameras with camera projection centers \(C\) and \(C'\), a 3D world point \(P \in \mathbb{R}^3\) is projected onto their corresponding image planes as image points
  • \(\tilde{p} = \begin{pmatrix}x & y & 1 \end{pmatrix}^\top\)
  • \(\tilde{p}' = \begin{pmatrix}x' & y' & 1 \end{pmatrix}^\top\)
Each image plane has a principal point, denoted by \((c_x,c_y), (c_x',c_y')\). It is the point where the optical axis intersects the image plane. The distance between the camera projection centers and the principal points is given by the focal length \(f\).

The Epipolar Constraint

The two camera projection centers \(C\) and \(C'\), together with the world point \(P\), span the epipolar plane. The relative pose between the two cameras is described by a rotation matrix \(R \in SO(3)\) and a translation vector \(t \in \mathbb{R}^3\).
  • \(R_{W \to L} = I\)
  • \(t_{W \to L} = (0,0,0)^\top\)
  • \(R_{W \to R} = R\)
  • \(t_{W \to R} = t\)
A 3D point \(P\) expressed in the coordinate system of the left camera can be transformed into the coordinate system of the right camera via \[ \begin{align*} P' &= RP + t \\ t \times P' &= t \times (RP + t) \\ t \times P' &= t \times RP + \underbrace{t \times t}_{=0} \\ \underbrace{P' \cdot (t \times P')}_{=0} &= P' \cdot (t \times RP) \\ 0 &= P' \cdot (t_\times R P) \\ 0 &= P' \cdot EP \\ 0 &= P'^\top E P \end{align*} \]
